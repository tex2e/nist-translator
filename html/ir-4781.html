INTRODUCTION

原本 PDF p.90

はじめに

In September 1980, as a result of an effort by the world's industrialized nations to arrive at a framework for protecting international flows of personal information, the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD) adopted "Guidelines Governing the Protection of Privacy and Transborder Flows of Personal Data" (Guidelines).1 The Guidelines recommend that, in connection with the collection, use, and disclosure of personal data, signatory countries adhere to eight principles, described as "minimum standards which are capable of being supplemented by additional measures for the protection of privacy and individual liberties."2 The Guidelines are applicable to both governmental and private records systems. The principles they incorporate, which have been variously described as standards of "data protection," "information privacy," and "fair information practice," have won increasing acceptance as basic rules for the management of personal data in the industrialized nations.

原本 PDF p.90

1980 年 9 月、世界の工業国が個人情報の国際的な流通を保護する枠組みに到達しようと努めた結果として、経済協力開発機構（OECD）は「プライバシー保護と個人データの国際流通についてのガイドライン」（以下「ガイドライン」）を採択しました。1 このガイドラインは、個人データの収集、利用、開示に関して、署名国が 8 つの原則を遵守することを勧告しており、これらは「プライバシーと個人の自由を保護するための追加的な措置によって補完しうる最低限の基準」と説明されています。2 ガイドラインは、政府と民間の双方の記録システムに適用されます。ガイドラインが取り入れている原則は、「データ保護」、「情報プライバシー」、「公正な情報慣行」の基準などさまざまに表現されてきましたが、工業国で個人データを管理するための基本規則として、受け入れが広がっています。

Paragraph 19 of the Guidelines recommends that in implementing the principles of privacy protection, "member countries establish legal, administrative or other procedures or institutions for the protection of privacy and individual liberties in respect of personal data." In the United States, such implementation depends on legal requirements as well as self-regulation, or voluntary practice. The legal aspects of privacy protection -- the laws currently contributing to implementation of privacy principles in the United States -- are the subject of this report.

原本 PDF p.90

ガイドラインの第 19 項は、プライバシー保護の原則を実施するにあたり、「加盟国は、個人データに関するプライバシーと個人の自由を保護するための、法的、行政的その他の手続または制度を確立する」ことを勧告しています。米国では、そのような実施は、自主規制、すなわち任意の実務に加えて、法的要件にも依存しています。プライバシー保護の法的側面、すなわち現在米国でプライバシー原則の実施に寄与している法律が、本報告書の主題です。

Privacy law in the United States is characterized by unusual diversity, as compared with the unitary schemes of regulation adopted by many countries of continental Europe. This report is not intended to be an exhaustive survey of U.S. laws. Instead, the purpose of the report is to illustrate the salient categories of privacy law in the United States, and to explain some of the factors contributing to its great variety and its differential treatment of governmental and private information practices. Particularly for readers who are unfamiliar with the development of U.S. privacy law, an awareness of its varied roots and diverse functions may help in understanding how this complex body of law applies to specific privacy problems and to implementation of the Guidelines principles.

原本 PDF p.90

米国のプライバシー法は、欧州大陸の多くの国が採用している統一的な規制の枠組みと比べて、並外れた多様性を特徴としています。本報告書は、米国の法律を網羅的に調査することを意図したものではありません。その目的は、米国のプライバシー法の顕著な類型を示し、その多様性の大きさと、政府と民間の情報慣行の取扱いの違いに寄与している要因をいくつか説明することです。特に米国のプライバシー法の発展に詳しくない読者にとっては、その多様な起源と機能を知ることが、この複雑な法体系が具体的なプライバシー問題やガイドラインの原則の実施にどう適用されるかを理解する助けになるでしょう。

The main text of the report consists of three sections. In the first section, the diversity of U.S. privacy law is analyzed in terms of the law's sources, content, and means of enforcement. The second and third sections compare the law's treatment of privacy problems in the public and private sectors, respectively. Supplementing the main text are two appendices. Appendix I, a "Partial List of United States Privacy Laws," breaks down representative statutes, constitutional requirements, and common law principles covering the public and private sectors according to the groups of Guidelines principles to which they primarily apply. The vast majority of U.S. privacy laws were developed or enacted prior to the issuance of the Guidelines, and many of them incorporate a different conceptual framework from that of the Guidelines. Appendix I is intended to help in matching up U.S. privacy laws to relevant Guidelines principles.

本報告書の本文は 3 つの節で構成されています。第 1 節では、米国のプライバシー法の多様性を、法の淵源、内容、執行手段の観点から分析します。第 2 節と第 3 節では、公的部門と民間部門のそれぞれにおけるプライバシー問題の法による取扱いを比較します。本文を補うものとして、2 つの付録があります。付録 I「米国のプライバシー法の一部一覧（Partial List of United States Privacy Laws）」は、公的部門と民間部門を対象とする代表的な制定法、憲法上の要件、コモンローの原則を、主に適用されるガイドラインの原則のグループ別に分類しています。米国のプライバシー法の大多数は、ガイドラインが出される前に策定または制定されたものであり、その多くはガイドラインとは異なる概念的な枠組みを取り入れています。付録 I は、米国のプライバシー法を、関連するガイドラインの原則に対応づける助けとなることを意図しています。

Appendix II, "Descriptions of Selected Privacy Laws," discusses in greater detail the specific requirements of representative privacy laws. This appendix focuses on laws covering the private sector, because in this sphere the absence of an omnibus statute such as the Privacy Act of 1974 adds to the complexity of the task of identifying relevant laws. There is a considerable body of privacy law that regulates private record-keeping, but it is not all found in one place. Appendix II is intended as a guide to the main categories of private sector privacy law.

I. DIVERSITY OF THE LAW

原本 PDF p.92

I. 法の多様性

Privacy law in the the United States is not limited to the Privacy Act of 1974: the law has sources in the Constitution and the common law as well as in legislation, and it includes statutes enacted by the States as well by the national legislature. The content of U.S. privacy law also varies considerably, depending on the type of record-keeping activity to which it applies. Finally, a number of different mechanisms are used to enforce these diverse legal requirements. In this section of the report, the variations in the sources, content, and methods of enforcement of U.S. privacy law are examined.

原本 PDF p.92

米国のプライバシー法は、1974 年プライバシー法（Privacy Act of 1974）に限られません。法の淵源は、立法のほか憲法とコモンローにもあり、国の立法府だけでなく州が制定した制定法も含まれます。米国のプライバシー法の内容も、適用される記録保持活動の種類によってかなり異なります。さらに、こうした多様な法的要件を執行するために、複数の異なる仕組みが用いられています。本報告書のこの節では、米国のプライバシー法の淵源、内容、執行方法の違いを検討します。

Diversity of Sources
原本 PDF p.92
淵源の多様性

The U.S. law of privacy is derived from a variety of sources, including the U.S. Constitution, the common law, and statutes and regulations at both Federal and State levels. This diversity reflects the historical process of accretion by which the law has developed, as well as the division of law-making authority, under the Federalist system, between the national government and the States.

原本 PDF p.92

米国のプライバシー法は、合衆国憲法、コモンロー、連邦・州の両レベルの制定法と規則など、さまざまな淵源に由来します。この多様性は、法が発展してきた歴史的な積み重ねの過程と、連邦制のもとで国と州の間に立法権限が分割されていることを反映しています。

The roots of privacy protection law in this country are found in the Constitution and the common law as well as in early statutes. Although the word "privacy" is nowhere mentioned in the Constitution, that document has historically played -- and continues to play -- a major part in protecting the values of privacy and liberty which are also the main concern of the OECD Guidelines. Among the fundamental safeguards established by the U.S. Constitution are guarantees of political and religious freedom, prohibitions against "unreasonable searches and seizures," and requirements for procedural fairness, or "due process of law." As the "supreme law of the land," such constitutional requirements can be repealed or superseded only by amendment of the Constitution itself, and they invalidate any conflicting statute. Moreover, the importance of the Constitution goes beyond its specific legal application, as constitutional principles have strongly influenced the drafting of privacy legislation.

原本 PDF p.92

この国のプライバシー保護法の根源は、初期の制定法のほか、憲法とコモンローにあります。憲法には「プライバシー」という語はどこにも出てきませんが、この文書は、OECD ガイドラインの主な関心事でもあるプライバシーと自由という価値を保護するうえで、歴史的に大きな役割を果たしており、今も果たし続けています。合衆国憲法が定める基本的な保護には、政治的・宗教的な自由の保障、「不合理な捜索および押収」の禁止、手続上の公正さ、すなわち「適正手続（due process of law）」の要件があります。「国の最高法規」として、このような憲法上の要件は、憲法自体の修正によってのみ廃止または置き換えができ、これに抵触する制定法は無効となります。さらに、憲法上の原則がプライバシー関連の立法の起草に強い影響を与えてきたため、憲法の重要性はその具体的な法的適用にとどまりません。

Privacy safeguards are also found in the "common law," a body of legal rules which originated in historical usages and customs and in the British laws given recognition by early U.S. courts, and which is continuously evolving through judicial interpretation. Common law may exist independently of any statute, though it is frequently modified or superseded by legislation. At the end of the nineteenth century, legal theorists proposed recognition of a general common law right to privacy, and since then the vast majority of States have recognized such a cause of action. In addition, the common law has provided basic privacy protections in such areas as defamation and breach of confidence.

原本 PDF p.93

プライバシーの保護は、「コモンロー」にも見られます。コモンローは、歴史的な慣行や慣習、および初期の米国の裁判所が認めた英国法に由来する法規則の集合であり、司法による解釈を通じて絶えず発展しています。コモンローは制定法とは独立に存在する場合がありますが、立法によって修正または置き換えられることも多くあります。19 世紀末に、法理論家はプライバシーの一般的なコモンロー上の権利の承認を提案し、それ以来、大多数の州がそのような訴訟原因を認めてきました。加えて、コモンローは、名誉毀損や信頼違反といった分野で、基本的なプライバシー保護を提供してきました。

Statutory law has played an increasingly important role in the development of U.S. privacy law. While serving to clarify and redefine the rights of individuals, new privacy statutes do not always supplant previous layers of statutory, constitutional, and common law. In banking, for example, the Right to Financial Privacy Act was not intended to replace the banker's common law duty of confidentiality, but to build upon it in order to address a new problem, the increasing collection of information from bank records by the Federal government. Similarly, enactment of the Privacy Act of 1974 left intact many earlier privacy-related statutes such as those covering census and tax data.

原本 PDF p.93

制定法は、米国のプライバシー法の発展において、ますます重要な役割を果たしてきました。新しいプライバシー関連の制定法は、個人の権利を明確にし再定義する一方で、それ以前の制定法、憲法、コモンローの各層を常に置き換えるとは限りません。たとえば銀行業では、金融プライバシー権法（Right to Financial Privacy Act）は、銀行家のコモンロー上の守秘義務を置き換えることを意図したものではなく、連邦政府による銀行記録からの情報収集の増加という新しい問題に対処するために、それを土台に築かれたものです。同様に、1974 年プライバシー法（Privacy Act of 1974）の制定は、国勢調査データや税データを扱うものなど、それ以前のプライバシー関連の制定法の多くをそのまま残しました。

In addition to its long history of development, a second important diversifying influence on U.S. privacy law is the Federalist system of government in the United States. Although the concerns of the national government have expanded greatly over the last 50 years, there remains a strong presumption in our political culture that problems should be solved at the State level whenever it is practical to do so. A wide variety of privacy statutes have been enacted by the States, and responsibility for interpreting and applying common-law safeguards lies primarily with State courts.

原本 PDF p.93

長い発展の歴史に加えて、米国のプライバシー法を多様化させている第 2 の重要な影響は、米国の連邦制の統治制度です。国の関心事は過去 50 年間に大きく広がりましたが、実務上可能であれば州レベルで問題を解決すべきであるという強い推定が、私たちの政治文化には残っています。州によって多種多様なプライバシー関連の制定法が制定されており、コモンロー上の保護を解釈し適用する責任は、主に州の裁判所にあります。

The relationship between State and Federal legislative jurisdiction contributes substantially to the complexity of U.S. privacy laws. In some areas, Federal law has "preempted the field," invalidating all State laws pertaining to the subject in question. More frequently, State and Federal laws operate in tandem: when a Federal statute is enacted, Congress may explicitly or implicitly leave room for State laws to go further, and for State administrative authorities to supplement Federal enforcement.

Diversity characterizes not only the sources, but also the type of protection offered by U.S. privacy laws. It is also significant that, in applying theories of privacy and related issues to specific record-keeping problems, U.S. lawmakers have on the whole taken a pragmatic approach. Many U.S. privacy safeguards have been tailored primarily to the record-keeping function affected and to the nature of the perceived threat to an individual's interests, rather than to a general framework of record-keeping obligations. As a result, the legal rules for the application of privacy principles are not necessarily the same for all record-keeping activities. Privacy, no less than other issues, is subject to considerations of legislative economy -- that schemes of government regulation should not be needlessly established or extended. The absence of a single legal standard covering all record-keeping activity in the United States reflects a widely held belief that such a law would lead to far more government regulation than is desirable in this field.

The third aspect of diversity in U.S. privacy laws is their means of enforcement. Privacy laws are enforced by individual lawsuits in court as well as by a variety of regulatory agencies at both the Federal and State levels. II. プライバシー法と政府4

The centerpiece of U.S. privacy law affecting government record-keeping is the Privacy Act of 1974. Like the omnibus "data protection" laws enacted by many European nations, the Privacy Act is a response to the growing use of computers to handle personal data and to the tremendous expansion of the national government's role in the lives of individuals. Government programs such as welfare assistance, social security, and medical and education assistance have required the government to amass huge quantities of personal information. At the same time, the sources of personal data accessible to the government have greatly expanded, with the growth of information-intensive services such as banking, credit, and insurance.

原本 PDF p.98

政府の記録保持に影響する米国のプライバシー法の中心にあるのは、1974 年プライバシー法（Privacy Act of 1974）です。多くのヨーロッパ諸国が制定した包括的な「データ保護」法と同様に、プライバシー法（Privacy Act）は、個人データを扱うコンピューターの利用の増加と、個人の生活における連邦政府の役割の大幅な拡大に対応したものです。福祉援助、社会保障、医療・教育援助などの政府プログラムによって、政府は膨大な量の個人情報を集めなければならなくなりました。同時に、銀行、信用、保険といった情報集約型のサービスが成長し、政府がアクセスできる個人データの情報源は大幅に拡大しました。

Unlike most of the U.S. privacy laws discussed in this paper, the Privacy Act incorporates in its entirety a theoretical framework that was developed explicitly to address the problems posed by computers and impersonal record-keeping institutions. From this theory of "fair information practice" -- the basic principles of which are also found in the OECD Guidelines and in many of Europe's data protection laws -- virtually all of the Privacy Act's requirements are directly derived.5 Covering the vast majority of personal records systems maintained by the Federal government, the Privacy Act requires Federal agencies to publish annually a description of their personal records systems, and to notify individuals, when collecting information from them, of the purposes for which it will be used. Limits are placed on the type of information collected and the method of collection. Personal information may be disclosed, as a general rule, only with the individual's consent or for purposes announced in advance. The Act provides standards for accuracy and relevance as well as the security of personal data. And finally, individuals are entitled to inspect, copy, and challenge the contents of records about themselves.

However, the Privacy Act is by no means the sole source of privacy safeguards applying to government. Beginning with the Constitution, a large body of law has developed in the United States to regulate the information practices of government agencies, and to prevent them from using personal information in ways detrimental to basic liberties. The process of expanding and strengthening Constitutional guarantees of privacy and liberty in response to new technology and new uses of records has been a recurrent one in U.S. history. In earlier years, innovations such as the telephone and telegraph, as well as the growth of information-intensive government functions such as the population census and tax collection, led to legislation and court decisions which aimed to prevent such changes from undermining the balance of power between citizens and government. These accumulated layers of law reflect deep-seated concerns that arbitrary actions of government pose the greatest threat to personal privacy and liberty.

Although the formal structure of the Privacy Act is new, the concerns underlying its enactment are very old by U.S. standards. Moreover, the background of Constitutional and legal theory from which the main principles of the Privacy Act are drawn includes not only traditional privacy rights, but also rights to non-discriminatory treatment and to procedural fairness, or "due process." These historical strands of which the theory of "fair information practice" is woven have continued to develop on their own in our legal system, creating independent networks of rights and obligations that overlap with the framework established by the Privacy Act.

Traditional Rights of Privacy
原本 PDF p.100
伝統的なプライバシー権

Many of the legal requirements supplementing the Privacy Act incorporate traditional ideas of privacy and confidentiality. Found in the Constitution as well as numerous statutes, such laws place additional and often quite strict limits on collection and disclosure of personal data by the government. The Fourth and Fifth Amendments to the Constitution, for example, have long provided a sphere of privacy around the individual that limits the methods used by the government to collect information about him or her. The Fourth Amendment protects "the right of the people to be secure in their persons, houses, papers, and effects, against unreasonable searches and seizures. . . . " The scope of these protected areas is today defined as those in which the individual "has a reasonable expectation of privacy," and includes technologically defined "zones of privacy" such as telephone transmissions, as well as the individual's person and property. As a general rule, the government cannot intrude into these areas to collect information without the individual's consent unless a "neutral and detached magistrate" has made a finding of "probable cause" that the evidence sought is relevant to an offense and is likely to be found in the specific places to be searched. The Fifth Amendment's privilege against self-incrimination provides a different sort of protection: it absolutely prohibits the government from compelling individuals themselves to provide information that would implicate them in criminal activity.

原本 PDF p.100

プライバシー法（Privacy Act）を補完する法的要件の多くは、プライバシーと守秘に関する伝統的な考え方を取り入れています。憲法や数多くの制定法に見られるこのような法律は、政府による個人データの収集と開示に、追加の、しばしば非常に厳格な制限を課しています。たとえば、合衆国憲法の修正第 4 条と第 5 条は、長年にわたり、個人の周囲にプライバシーの領域を確保し、政府がその個人に関する情報を収集するために用いる方法を制限してきました。修正第 4 条は、「不合理な捜索および押収に対し、身体、住居、書類および所持品の安全を保障される人民の権利」を保護しています。保護されるこれらの領域の範囲は、今日では、個人が「プライバシーについての合理的な期待をもつ」領域と定義され、個人の身体や財産のほか、電話の通信など技術的に定義された「プライバシーの領域」も含みます。原則として、政府は、「中立で公正な裁判官」が、求める証拠が犯罪に関連し、捜索する特定の場所で見つかる可能性が高いという「相当な理由（probable cause）」を認定しない限り、本人の同意なくこれらの領域に立ち入って情報を収集することはできません。修正第 5 条の自己負罪拒否特権は、別の種類の保護を与えます。これは、犯罪行為に関与していることを示す情報を、個人自身に提供するよう政府が強制することを、絶対的に禁じています。

While demarcating a zone of privacy around the individual, these Constitutional requirements do not protect that zone completely: in the case of the Fourth Amendment, for example, persons and property may be searched upon a showing of "probable cause;" in the case of the Fifth Amendment, criminal investigations may be conducted and evidence obtained as long as the individual himself is not forced to divulge incriminating information. In effect, the Fourth and Fifth Amendments serve to assure "fair play" when the government intrudes on individual privacy.

原本 PDF p.101

これらの憲法上の要件は、個人の周囲にプライバシーの領域を画定しますが、その領域を完全に保護するわけではありません。たとえば、修正第 4 条の場合、「相当な理由」が示されれば、人と財産は捜索される可能性があります。修正第 5 条の場合、個人自身が罪に問われうる情報の開示を強制されない限り、犯罪捜査を行い、証拠を入手することができます。実際には、修正第 4 条と第 5 条は、政府が個人のプライバシーに介入する際に「公正なやり方（fair play）」を確保する役割を果たしています。

As government has increased its involvement in areas of private life formerly considered solely the business of the individual, new safeguards have been enacted to protect privately held institutional records, which provide extensive documentation of an individual's personal affairs but which are not nearly as well protected by the Constitution as are an individual's private papers. For example, the Right to Financial Privacy Act of 1978 provides a mechanism regulating government access to bank records. Similarly, the Fair Credit Reporting Act of 1970 limits government access to information maintained by consumer reporting agencies, and requires a court order when information is acquired by the government for other than business purposes. Access by the Internal Revenue Service to certain records maintained by private recordkeepers is limited by the Tax Reform Act of 1976, which like the Right to Financial Privacy Act includes a requirement for individuals to receive notice of and an opportunity to challenge disclosures.

原本 PDF p.101

政府が、かつては個人だけの問題と考えられていた私生活の領域に関与を強めるにつれ、民間が保有する機関の記録を保護するために、新たな保護措置が制定されました。これらの記録は、個人の私的な事柄を広範に記録していますが、憲法による保護は、個人の私的な書類ほど十分ではありません。たとえば、1978 年金融プライバシー権法（Right to Financial Privacy Act of 1978）は、政府による銀行記録へのアクセスを規制する仕組みを定めています。同様に、1970 年公正信用報告法（Fair Credit Reporting Act of 1970）は、消費者信用情報機関が保有する情報への政府のアクセスを制限し、政府が事業目的以外で情報を取得する場合に裁判所の命令を求めています。民間の記録保持者が保有する特定の記録への内国歳入庁のアクセスは、1976 年税制改革法（Tax Reform Act of 1976）によって制限されています。同法には、金融プライバシー権法（Right to Financial Privacy Act）と同様に、個人が開示の通知を受け、異議を申し立てる機会を得る要件が含まれています。

In addition to placing limits on the government's collection of information from private sources, traditional privacy requirements also supplement the Privacy Act's safeguards for non-disclosure, or confidentiality, of personal information maintained by the government in the course of its business. Privacy rights of this sort have been established in part as an extension of similar rights in the private sector (e.g., because information was obtained from confidential sources such as medical records) and in part as a means of assuring the cooperation of the public in a data gathering effort such as the population census or tax administration. Under the Privacy Act, the principle of confidentiality was generalized to cover the vast majority of government records, and to require that such records be disclosed only for publicly announced purposes. However, many kinds of government records continue to be covered by separate confidentiality statutes, which often impose much stricter limits on disclosure than those of the Privacy Act.

政府が民間の情報源から情報を収集することへの制限に加えて、伝統的なプライバシー要件は、政府が業務の過程で保有する個人情報の非開示、すなわち守秘に関するプライバシー法（Privacy Act）の保護措置も補完しています。この種のプライバシー権は、一つには、民間部門の同様の権利の延長として（たとえば、情報が医療記録のような秘密の情報源から得られたために）、また一つには、国勢調査や税務行政のようなデータ収集の取り組みに対する一般の協力を確保する手段として確立されました。プライバシー法（Privacy Act）のもとで、守秘の原則は政府の記録の大多数を対象とするものに一般化され、そのような記録を公に告知された目的のためにのみ開示することが求められました。しかし、多くの種類の政府の記録は、引き続き個別の守秘に関する制定法の対象となっており、それらはプライバシー法（Privacy Act）よりはるかに厳しい開示の制限を課していることが多くあります。

A second source of legal requirements supplementing the Privacy Act is the law relating to discrimination, which limits the use of "suspect" criteria in government decision-making. The Privacy Act and other comprehensive privacy statutes have borrowed less from this legal tradition than from others discussed in this paper. However, in view of the emphasis on non-discrimination in the OECD Guidelines,6 any discussion of their implementation in the United States must refer to this separate and quite extensive body of law.

Perhaps the best-known example of laws against discrimination is the equal protection clause of the Fourteenth Amendment to the Constitution (applying explicitly to the States, and to the Federal government by implication under the Fifth Amendment), which virtually bans the government from penalizing an individual because of race, color, or national origin, and which sharply limits discrimination on the basis of sex, illegitimacy, or alien status. Somewhat analogous limits on discrimination are established by the First Amendment to the Constitution, which restricts adverse treatment of individuals because of their political and religious beliefs and associations.

Rights to Procedural Fairness
原本 PDF p.103
手続上の公正さに対する権利

A third source of rights supplementing those of the Privacy Act is the principle of "due process," which requires a fair opportunity for individuals to contest government action taken or proposed to be taken against them. Under the Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments to the Constitution, "due process" requirements originally applied to government action depriving the individual of liberty or property; as a result of expanding judicial interpretation of the due process clause and the enactment of statutes such as the Administrative Procedure Act, "due process" or procedural fairness (in the form of adequate notice and an opportunity to be heard in one's own defense) applies to a wide variety of government decisions in which individuals have a stake. Before such decisions can become final, the individual is entitled, among other things, to scrutinize and challenge the factual record on which it is based.

プライバシー法（Privacy Act）の権利を補完する権利の第 3 の淵源は、「適正手続」の原則であり、個人に対してとられた、またはとられる予定の政府の行為を争う公正な機会を求めます。合衆国憲法修正第 5 条と第 14 条のもとで、「適正手続」の要件は、当初は個人の自由や財産を奪う政府の行為に適用されていました。適正手続条項に対する司法解釈の拡大と、行政手続法（Administrative Procedure Act）のような制定法の制定の結果、「適正手続」すなわち手続上の公正さ（十分な通知と、自らの弁明のために意見を述べる機会という形で）は、個人が利害関係をもつ多様な政府の決定に適用されています。そのような決定が確定する前に、個人は、とりわけ、その決定の根拠となる事実の記録を精査し、異議を申し立てる権利があります。

Allusions to the principle of due process pervade the legislative history of the Privacy Act.7 Although due process requirements have traditionally been triggered by specific actions or proposed actions of the government, those of the Privacy Act come into play whenever information is collected about an individual. One purpose of the Act's safeguards is to protect individuals when information about them is used to make informal, discretionary decisions of which they may be unaware. Specifically, individuals are entitled to learn why information is being collected about them and to inspect and dispute the contents of records. Another due process related objective of the Privacy Act is to assure that the less visible decision-making processes of government can be brought into the open. In addressing concerns about open government, the Privacy Act overlaps with other recently enacted Federal statutes such as the Freedom of Information Act and the Government-in-the-Sunshine Act.

III. PRIVACY LAW AND THE PRIVATE SECTOR8

原本 PDF p.104

III. プライバシー法と民間部門8

Like the law applying to government, the privacy law applying to the private sector has developed from a number of related legal traditions. Unlike the public sector, however, private record-keeping activity is not covered by a single, comprehensive privacy statute. There are a number of reasons for this state of affairs, some of which were outlined by the Privacy Protection Study Commission in explicitly rejecting a proposal for such a statute: (1) the greater influence on the private sector of economic incentives that encourage voluntary compliance with privacy principles; (2) the difficulty of legislating a single standard for widely varying record-keeping practices in the private sector, and (3) the danger of government control over private flows of information.9

原本 PDF p.105

政府に適用される法と同様に、民間部門に適用されるプライバシー法も、いくつかの関連する法的伝統から発展してきました。しかし、公的部門とは異なり、民間の記録保持活動は、単一の包括的なプライバシー関連の制定法の対象になっていません。このような状況には複数の理由があり、その一部は、そのような制定法の提案を明示的に退けたプライバシー保護研究委員会（Privacy Protection Study Commission）が概説しました。すなわち、(1) プライバシー原則の自発的な遵守を促す経済的インセンティブが、民間部門にはより大きく影響すること、(2) 民間部門の大きく異なる記録保持の慣行に対して、単一の基準を立法化することが困難であること、(3) 民間の情報の流れに対する政府の管理の危険性、です。9

In addition, the accumulation of personal data in the private sector is not widely perceived to be as inherently dangerous to fundamental liberties as in the case of government records. Although private sector organizations collect vast quantities of personal information about individuals, and although the use of such information can have serious consequences, the coercive powers of government are not available to such organizations. In the view of many, therefore, the mere presence of personal data in private files does not constitute as great a threat. Accordingly, private sector safeguards are more likely to be tailored to the type of records involved and the nature of the harm caused by their misuse.

原本 PDF p.105

加えて、民間部門での個人データの蓄積は、政府の記録の場合ほど基本的自由に本質的に危険であるとは広く認識されていません。民間部門の組織は個人に関する膨大な量の個人情報を収集しており、その情報の使用が深刻な結果をもたらしうるとしても、そのような組織には政府の強制権限はありません。そのため、多くの人の見方では、民間のファイルに個人データが存在するだけでは、さほど大きな脅威にはなりません。したがって、民間部門の保護措置は、関係する記録の種類と、その悪用によって生じる損害の性質に合わせて仕立てられることが多くなります。

The development of privacy law in the private sector, then, has been a selective process. In addition to the incremental evolution of common-law safeguards, much of the law applying to private record-keeping has accumulated through enactment of specifically targeted statutes, usually following a sharply focused legislative inquiry that identifies problems arising in a particular record-keeping relationship. Banks, employers, schools, and insurance companies each play a distinct role in the lives of individuals. Those roles largely determine the contents of personal records and how they are used. Accordingly, privacy protection requirements for the private sector vary significantly from one record-keeping relationship to another: what is needed to protect bank records may not be appropriate for insurance or employment records.

原本 PDF p.105

そうすると、民間部門でのプライバシー法の発展は、選択的な過程でした。コモンロー上の保護措置が少しずつ発展してきたことに加えて、民間の記録保持に適用される法の多くは、特定の対象を絞った制定法の制定を通じて蓄積されてきました。通常は、特定の記録保持関係で生じる問題を明らかにする、焦点を絞った立法調査が先行しました。銀行、雇用主、学校、保険会社は、それぞれ個人の生活で異なる役割を果たしています。それらの役割が、個人記録の内容とその使われ方を大きく左右します。したがって、民間部門のプライバシー保護要件は、記録保持関係ごとに大きく異なります。銀行の記録を保護するために必要なものが、保険や雇用の記録には適切でない場合があります。

Some of the privacy law applying to the private sector does take the form of comprehensive statutes, similar to the Privacy Act of 1974, which apply a broad set of privacy principles to particular record-keeping industries. The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act, the National Association of Insurance Commissioners' Insurance Information and Privacy Protection Model Act (enacted in ten States since its approval by the Commissioners at the end of 1979), and the Fair Credit Reporting Act are examples of such comprehensive laws. The latter statute covers not only credit bureaus, but a variety of other information brokers that supply personal data about applicants for insurance, employment, government licenses, and other benefits. In order to protect individuals and allow them to interact with such organizations, with whom most people ordinarily have no direct contact, the Fair Credit Reporting Act sets standards for the collection, maintenance, and disclosure of personal data, and provides record-access and correction rights analogous to those of the Privacy Act. Comprehensive statutes of this sort are usually enforced through a combination of administrative supervision by existing regulatory authorities and private rights of action in court by the individuals themselves.

原本 PDF p.105

民間部門に適用されるプライバシー法の一部は、特定の記録保持産業に広範なプライバシー原則の集合を適用する、1974 年プライバシー法（Privacy Act of 1974）に類似した包括的な制定法の形をとっています。家族教育権・プライバシー法（Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act）、全米保険監督官協会（National Association of Insurance Commissioners）の保険情報・プライバシー保護モデル法（Insurance Information and Privacy Protection Model Act）（1979 年末に同協会が承認して以来、10 州で制定されています）、公正信用報告法（Fair Credit Reporting Act）は、このような包括的な法律の例です。公正信用報告法（Fair Credit Reporting Act）は、信用調査機関だけでなく、保険、雇用、政府の免許、その他の給付の申請者に関する個人データを提供するさまざまな情報仲介業者も対象としています。大半の人が通常は直接の接点をもたないこうした組織から個人を保護し、個人がそれらの組織と関わることができるようにするため、公正信用報告法（Fair Credit Reporting Act）は、個人データの収集、維持、開示の基準を定め、プライバシー法（Privacy Act）に類似した、記録へのアクセスと訂正の権利を定めています。この種の包括的な制定法は、通常、既存の規制当局による行政上の監督と、個人自身による裁判所での私的な訴権とを組み合わせて執行されます。

In contrast to comprehensive statutes, many of the laws applying to the private sector are not explicitly identified as privacy protection laws; nevertheless, they play a major role in furthering implementation of the Guidelines principles. Such requirements are found in diverse places, including consumer protection, equal opportunity, and confidentiality statutes; common-law tort principles such as defamation and intrusion; and express or implied contracts between banker and customer or doctor and patient. Many of these laws focus on particular kinds of record-keeping activity that pose an identifiable threat of harm to the individual. For example, the Fair Credit Billing Act, which establishes detailed requirements for the resolution of billing disputes in open-ended installment credit accounts, equips individuals to protect their privacy as well as financial interests in a specific type of situation for which strong procedural safeguards have been deemed necessary. Others provide a specific type of broadly applicable right --for example, against intrusive data collection methods or unfair discrimination.

原本 PDF p.106

包括的な制定法とは対照的に、民間部門に適用される法律の多くは、プライバシー保護法として明示的に位置づけられていません。それでも、ガイドラインの原則の実施を進めるうえで大きな役割を果たしています。このような要件は、消費者保護、機会均等、守秘に関する制定法、名誉毀損や侵入といったコモンロー上の不法行為の原則、銀行家と顧客、医師と患者の間の明示的または黙示的な契約など、さまざまなところに見られます。これらの法律の多くは、個人に危害を及ぼす明確な脅威をもたらす特定の種類の記録保持活動に焦点を当てています。たとえば、公正信用請求法（Fair Credit Billing Act）は、残高繰越型の分割払信用口座における請求紛争の解決について詳細な要件を定め、強力な手続上の保護措置が必要と判断された特定の状況において、個人が財産上の利益だけでなくプライバシーも守れるようにしています。ほかの法律は、たとえば侵入的なデータ収集方法や不公正な差別に対する、広く適用される特定の種類の権利を定めています。

Although the Constitution itself does not directly apply to private-sector record-keeping, the development of privacy law for that sector has been subject to the influence of Constitutional principles. As a result, there are many points of similarity between the legal requirements affecting government and private-sector record-keeping, and this paper uses the same categories in classifying them. In addition to the comprehensive statutes discussed above, the law of private record-keeping includes (1) traditional rights of privacy and confidentiality, as developed by the common law and supplemented by statute; (2) rights against discrimination by race and other suspect categories; and (3) rights to procedural fairness in institutional decision-making.

原本 PDF p.107

Traditional Rights of Privacy
原本 PDF p.107
伝統的なプライバシー権

The phrase "right to be let alone" was used by nineteenth-century American jurists to denote a variety of common-law principles then in force to protect various aspects of personal privacy.10 Since that time, a number of common-law and statutory rights have been developed to protect the individual's interests in seclusion, the keeping of secrets, and reputation -- the interests which in ordinary language are associated with the word "privacy." The development of legal safeguards for these interests has a long and unsystematic history, and the description here will be confined to enumeration of the main branches of the law.

原本 PDF p.107

「ひとりで放っておいてもらう権利（right to be let alone）」という表現は、19 世紀の米国の法学者が、当時施行されていた、個人のプライバシーのさまざまな側面を保護するためのさまざまなコモンロー上の原則を示すために用いました。10 それ以来、人目を避けて隔離された状態にあること、秘密の保持、名誉に対する個人の利益、つまり日常的な言葉では「プライバシー」という語と結びつけられる利益を保護するために、多数のコモンロー上および制定法上の権利が発展してきました。これらの利益のための法的保護措置の発展には、長く体系化されていない歴史があり、ここでは法の主な分野を列挙するにとどめます。

One branch of the common law of privacy is traceable to the laws of trespass, assault, and battery. The subcategory of the invasion-of-privacy tort known as "intrusion on physical solitude and seclusion" allows the collection of damages for invasion of one's person, property, and other "zones" of privacy, even though no physical damage has resulted.

原本 PDF p.107

プライバシーに関するコモンローの一つの分野は、不法侵入、暴行、傷害に関する法にさかのぼることができます。プライバシー侵害の不法行為のうち、「身体的な孤独と隔離への侵入」として知られる小分類では、物理的な損害が生じていなくても、個人の身体、財産、その他のプライバシーの「領域」への侵害について損害賠償を得ることができます。

A second branch of the law is rooted in the law of defamation, and protects against public disclosure of embarrassing private facts. Although the torts of defamation and public disclosure are related, it is important to distinguish the two. One obvious difference is that defamation involves statements that are untrue, while the privacy tort of public disclosure involves statements that, even though true, are inappropriate for publication. In addition, the level of publicity required to make defamation actionable is considerably lower than that required for a public disclosure action.

原本 PDF p.108

法の第 2 の分野は、名誉毀損法に根ざし、恥ずかしい私的な事実が公表されることから保護するものです。名誉毀損と私的事実の公表に関する不法行為は関連していますが、両者を区別することが重要です。明らかな違いの一つは、名誉毀損が真実でない陳述を対象とするのに対し、私的事実の公表というプライバシー侵害の不法行為は、真実であっても公表するのが不適切な陳述を対象とすることです。加えて、名誉毀損に基づいて訴訟を提起するために必要な公表の範囲は、私的事実の公表に基づく訴訟に必要な範囲よりもかなり狭いです。

The third branch of common law privacy rights involves the keeping of secrets -- usually the confidences resulting from a professional or service relationship. This branch establishes the liability of physicians, attorneys, bankers, and others for unauthorized disclosure of confidential information entrusted to them. Common-law principles of confidentiality have frequently been codified in statutes -- in some cases merely stating the general principle, in others specifying authorized disclosures in some detail, including the inadmissibility of such information in court absent a waiver of its confidential status. Common law privacy safeguards and their statutory offspring are primarily enforced in the courts; however, in the case of professional confidences, either explicit or implicit enforcement powers are often vested in State licensing boards, and sometimes in Federal administrative bodies.

原本 PDF p.108

コモンロー上のプライバシー権の第 3 の分野は、秘密の保持、通常は専門的関係やサービス関係から生じる信頼に関するものです。この分野は、医師、弁護士、銀行家などが、託された秘密情報を無許可で開示した場合の責任を定めています。守秘に関するコモンロー上の原則は、制定法に成文化されていることが多くあります。一般原則を述べるだけのものもあれば、機密としての地位が放棄されていない限りそのような情報が裁判で証拠として認められないことを含め、許可される開示を詳細に定めるものもあります。コモンロー上のプライバシー保護措置とその制定法上の派生物は、主に裁判所で執行されます。ただし、専門家の秘密の場合は、明示的または黙示的な執行権限が州の免許委員会に与えられていることが多く、連邦の行政機関に与えられることもあります。

Rights Against Discrimination
原本 PDF p.108
差別に対する権利

A second major category of protections are those provided by equal opportunity statutes at the Federal and State level. Enacted to prevent discrimination with a serious impact on individual welfare, these statutes apply primarily to decisions on matters of economic importance to the individual. Examples at the Federal level include the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, the Equal Employment Opportunity Act, and the Fair Housing statute. Administrative bodies have played a major role in the enforcement of equal opportunity laws, in part because proof of unfair discrimination often emerges only as a "pattern or practice" determined on the basis of statistical evidence covering many individual cases.

原本 PDF p.109

保護の第 2 の主要な類型は、連邦および州レベルの機会均等に関する制定法によるものです。個人の福祉に深刻な影響を与える差別を防ぐために制定されたこれらの制定法は、主に、個人にとって経済的に重要な事項についての決定に適用されます。連邦レベルの例には、平等信用機会法（Equal Credit Opportunity Act）、平等雇用機会法（Equal Employment Opportunity Act）、公正住宅法（Fair Housing statute）があります。機会均等法の執行では、行政機関が主要な役割を果たしてきました。これは一つには、不公正な差別の証拠が、多数の個別事案を対象とする統計的証拠に基づいて判断される「パターンまたは慣行」として初めて明らかになることが多いためです。

Rights to Procedural Fairness
原本 PDF p.109
手続上の公正さに対する権利

A third category of private-sector law applies concepts of procedural fairness to private relationships. At the Federal level, such statutes apply mainly to the banking and credit sectors; however, there has been considerable legislative activity at the State level with respect to insurance, medical, and employment records. The main objective of such laws is to promote greater individual participation and, it is hoped, greater public confidence in private-sector decision-making processes, many of which are perceived as analogous to the operation of government bureaucracies. Examples of such laws at the Federal level are the Fair Credit Billing Act and the Electronic Funds Transfer Act, which attempt to promote fairness in the innovative but often impersonal relationships arising from the use of credit cards and electronic banking. Examples at the state level include statutes providing for individual rights of access to medical and employment records.

原本 PDF p.109

民間部門の法の第 3 の類型は、手続上の公正さの概念を私的な関係に適用するものです。連邦レベルでは、そのような制定法は主に銀行および信用の分野に適用されますが、州レベルでは、保険、医療、雇用の記録に関して、かなりの立法活動がありました。このような法律の主な目的は、個人の参加を促し、ひいては民間部門の意思決定過程に対する一般の信頼を高めることです。民間部門の意思決定過程の多くは、政府の官僚機構の運営に類似していると認識されています。連邦レベルのこのような法律の例には、公正信用請求法（Fair Credit Billing Act）と電子資金移動法（Electronic Funds Transfer Act）があり、クレジットカードや電子バンキングの利用から生じる、革新的だがしばしば非人格的な関係において公正さを促進しようとしています。州レベルの例には、医療記録や雇用記録に対する個人のアクセス権を定める制定法があります。

SUMMARY

原本 PDF p.109

まとめ

The body of law implementing privacy protection principles in the United States has evolved in diverse, multi-jurisdictional layers, reflecting our pragmatic, pluralistic system as well as an inclination to avoid centralized authority over personal data. Much of the law is rooted in Constitutional restrictions on the power of government, and in the individual's common-law "right to be let alone."

原本 PDF p.109

米国でプライバシー保護の原則を実施する法の体系は、多様で複数の管轄にまたがる層を成して発展してきました。それは、実際的で多元的な制度と、個人データに対する集中的な権限を避けようとする傾向を反映しています。法の多くは、政府の権力に対する憲法上の制限と、個人のコモンロー上の「ひとりで放っておいてもらう権利（right to be let alone）」に根ざしています。

In some areas, the source of protection is the Federal Congress and courts; however, the States have also acted to protect privacy in the many areas where they have traditionally asserted jurisdiction. As a result of the broad range of concerns covered by modern definitions of privacy, and the pragmatism that has informed the application of privacy principles, the content of privacy law varies widely for different kinds of record-keeping activity, with more comprehensive coverage of the government than of the private sector. The end result is a highly varied system of privacy law which nevertheless affords an extensive network of protections for the individual.

原本 PDF p.110

保護の淵源が連邦議会と連邦の裁判所である分野もあります。しかし、州も、伝統的に管轄を主張してきた多くの分野で、プライバシーを保護するために行動してきました。現代のプライバシーの定義が扱う懸念が広範であることと、プライバシー原則の適用を導いてきた実際的な姿勢の結果、プライバシー法の内容は、記録保持活動の種類によって大きく異なり、民間部門よりも政府のほうがより包括的に対象とされています。最終的な結果は、非常に多様なプライバシー法の体系ですが、それでも個人に対して広範な保護の網を提供しています。
