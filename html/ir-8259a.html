Abstract

原本 PDF p.4

概要

Device cybersecurity capabilities are cybersecurity features or functions that computing devices provide through their own technical means (i.e., device hardware and software). This publication defines an Internet of Things (IoT) device cybersecurity capability core baseline, which is a set of device capabilities generally needed to support common cybersecurity controls that protect an organization’s devices as well as device data, systems, and ecosystems. The purpose of this publication is to provide organizations a starting point to use in identifying the device cybersecurity capabilities for new IoT devices they will manufacture, integrate, or acquire. This publication can be used in conjunction with NISTIR 8259, Foundational Cybersecurity Activities for IoT Device Manufacturers.

原本 PDF p.4

1 Introduction

原本 PDF p.7

1 はじめに

Computing devices that integrate physical and/or sensing capabilities and network interface capabilities are being designed, developed, and deployed at an ever-increasing pace. These devices are fulfilling customer needs in all sectors of the economy. Many of these computing devices are connected to the internet. A novel characteristic of these devices is the combination of connectivity and the ability to sense and/or affect the physical world. As devices become smaller and more complex, with an increasing number of features, the security of those devices also becomes more complex. This publication defines a baseline set of device cybersecurity capabilities that organizations should consider when confronting the challenge of the Internet of Things (IoT).

原本 PDF p.7

物理的な機能やセンシング機能、およびネットワークインターフェース機能を統合したコンピューティングデバイスが、かつてない速さで設計、開発、配備されています。これらのデバイスは、経済のあらゆる分野で顧客のニーズを満たしています。これらのコンピューティングデバイスの多くは、インターネットに接続されています。これらのデバイスの新しい特徴は、接続性と、物理世界を感知する、または物理世界に作用する能力の組み合わせです。デバイスが小型化・複雑化し、機能の数が増えるにつれて、それらのデバイスのセキュリティも複雑になります。この刊行物は、組織がモノのインターネット（IoT）という課題に向き合う際に検討すべき、デバイスのサイバーセキュリティ能力のベースラインの集合を定義します。

Device cybersecurity capabilities are cybersecurity features or functions that computing devices provide through their own technical means (i.e., device hardware and software). The IoT device cybersecurity capability core baseline (core baseline)1 defined in this publication is a set of device capabilities generally needed to support commonly used cybersecurity controls that protect devices as well as device data, systems, and ecosystems. The concept of a baseline in any context requires careful consideration; security capabilities for IoT devices are no exception.

原本 PDF p.7

デバイスのサイバーセキュリティ能力とは、コンピューティングデバイスが自らの技術的手段（すなわち、デバイスのハードウェアとソフトウェア）によって提供する、サイバーセキュリティの特徴または機能です。この刊行物で定義する IoT デバイスのサイバーセキュリティ能力のコアベースライン（コアベースライン）1は、デバイスに加え、デバイスのデータ、システム、エコシステムを保護する、一般的に使われているサイバーセキュリティ管理策をサポートするために、一般に必要となるデバイス能力の集合です。どのような文脈でもベースラインという概念には慎重な検討が必要であり、IoT デバイスのセキュリティ能力も例外ではありません。

The core baseline has been derived from researching common cybersecurity risk management approaches and commonly used capabilities for addressing cybersecurity risks to IoT devices, which were refined and validated using a collaborative public-private process to incorporate all viewpoints. Multiple requests for comment were issued, and multiple workshops and roundtables were held. NIST is committed to an open and transparent process that facilitates stakeholder feedback and iterative improvement.

原本 PDF p.7

コアベースラインは、一般的なサイバーセキュリティリスクマネジメントのアプローチと、IoT デバイスに対するサイバーセキュリティリスクに対処するために一般的に使われている能力を調査して導き出され、あらゆる観点を取り入れるために、官民が協力するプロセスを使って洗練され、検証されました。複数回の意見募集が行われ、複数のワークショップと円卓会議が開催されました。NIST は、ステークホルダーのフィードバックと反復的な改善を促す、公開で透明なプロセスに取り組んでいます。

These capabilities were developed in the context of NISTIR 8259, Foundational Cybersecurity Activities for IoT Device Manufacturers [2], which discusses considerations for manufacturers to help guide them in choosing and implementing the device cybersecurity capabilities their IoT devices will provide. NISTIR 8259 also defines terminology and concepts that provide critical context for understanding device cybersecurity capabilities as one part of the entire IoT cybersecurity ecosystem. Thus, though both NISTIR 8259 and this publication have manufacturers as the intended audience, the considerations and capabilities discussed across the two publications can be used by manufacturers, integrators, or consumers. For more information on how these capabilities can be incorporated into a manufacturer’s development processes, see NISTIR 8259. Other organizations can use the core baseline in the context that is available and appropriate to them.

原本 PDF p.7

これらの能力は、NISTIR 8259, Foundational Cybersecurity Activities for IoT Device Manufacturers [2] の文脈で策定されました。この文書は、製造者が、自らの IoT デバイスが提供するデバイスのサイバーセキュリティ能力を選択し実装する際の指針となる考慮事項を論じています。NISTIR 8259 はまた、デバイスのサイバーセキュリティ能力を、IoT サイバーセキュリティエコシステム全体の一部として理解するために重要な文脈となる、用語と概念も定義しています。したがって、NISTIR 8259 とこの刊行物はどちらも製造者を想定読者としていますが、2 つの刊行物で論じられている考慮事項と能力は、製造者、インテグレーター、消費者が使用できます。これらの能力を製造者の開発プロセスに組み込む方法について詳しくは、NISTIR 8259 を参照してください。他の組織は、自らが利用でき、自らに適した文脈で、コアベースラインを使用できます。

Regardless of an organization’s role, this baseline is intended to give all organizations a starting point for IoT device cybersecurity risk management, but the implementation of all capabilities is not considered mandatory. The individual capabilities in the baseline may be implemented in full, in part, or not at all. It is left to the implementing organization to understand the unique risk context in which it operates and what is appropriate for its given circumstance. For more information on how to conduct a risk assessment, see NIST Special Publication 800-30, Guide for Conducting Risk Assessments [3].

原本 PDF p.7

組織の役割にかかわらず、このベースラインは、すべての組織に IoT デバイスのサイバーセキュリティリスクマネジメントの出発点を与えることを意図したものですが、すべての能力を実装することは必須とは見なされません。ベースラインの個々の能力は、完全に実装しても、部分的に実装しても、まったく実装しなくてもかまいません。自らが事業を行っている固有のリスクの文脈と、自らの状況に適したものが何であるかを理解することは、実装する組織に委ねられています。リスクアセスメントの実施方法について詳しくは、NIST Special Publication 800-30, Guide for Conducting Risk Assessments [3] を参照してください。

2 IoT Device Cybersecurity Capability Core Baseline Definition

原本 PDF p.9

2 IoT デバイスのサイバーセキュリティ能力のコアベースラインの定義

Table 1 defines the IoT device cybersecurity capability core baseline. The core baseline’s role is as a default for minimally securable devices. However, device cybersecurity capabilities will often need to be added or removed from an IoT device’s design, integration, or acquisition to best address an organization’s common cybersecurity risks. インターフェースへの論理アクセス: IoT デバイスは、そのローカルインターフェースおよびネットワークインターフェース、ならびにそれらのインターフェースが使うプロトコルとサービスへの論理アクセスを、認可されたエンティティのみに制限できます。

Though IoT devices providing the basic device cybersecurity capabilities described in the core baseline may help many customers more easily meet their cybersecurity needs and goals while using IoT devices, in reality, customers will likely target different and more specific risk mitigation areas. Therefore, the six capabilities and common elements in Table 1 should not be considered the universal and complete definition of necessary device cybersecurity capabilities for all customers.

As described in NISTIR 8259, manufacturers should consider their IoT device’s expected customers and expected use cases to begin to identify the precise device cybersecurity capabilities needed in context. Manufacturers can further define the device cybersecurity capabilities with new or additional elements based on their knowledge and research of their customers. This may mean incorporating device cybersecurity capabilities to support risk mitigations in other areas than what is discussed here (e.g., penetration or other forms of component testing/validation, specific network architectures to reduce risk) or incorporating more unique elements for an IoT device that support less broadly applicable, but in the context of a customer or use case vitally important risk mitigations and other cybersecurity needs and goals. Manufacturers should also keep in mind other considerations in addition to risk mitigations that may impact device cybersecurity and their elements, such as usability considerations based on the customer and use case, roles and responsibility related to cybersecurity and how customers may expect them to be distributed, and societal cybersecurity needs and goals (e.g., protection against the development of botnets) that may not be directly reflected in the customer’s needs and goals, to name a few.

Authorized EntityAn entity that has implicitly or explicitly been granted approval to interact with a particular IoT device.
