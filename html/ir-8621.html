Abstract

原本 PDF p.5

要旨

The NIST Workshop on SI Incident Management convened over 400 experts across government, frontier-model developers, cloud and cybersecurity providers, academia, standards organizations, and civil society to examine how organizations can prepare for, detect, handle, and recover from SI-related incidents. Motivated by federal directives, the workshop addressed the need for standards, response frameworks, and technical incident response capabilities as SI systems expand across critical infrastructure. To guide these efforts, participants framed SI incident management across two interrelated domains: cybersecurity attacks that target SI systems and incidents that stem from SI misuse, anomalous behavior, or severe operational malfunctions resulting in harm. Discussions and technical presentations highlighted the limitations of traditional, deterministic incident response life cycles when applied to probabilistic, autonomous, and deeply integrated SI systems. Participants emphasized critical operational needs, including reasoning-layer telemetry, non-human identity governance, authorization-scoped containment, clear evidentiary and reporting schemas, and coordinated responsibility across distributed SI value chains. This report synthesizes the core findings, practitioner insights, and actionable recommendations from the workshop to establish a strategic foundation for future NIST guidelines, operational playbooks, and updates to federal incident response frameworks.

1. Introduction

原本 PDF p.9

1. はじめに

Super intelligence (SI)1 is rapidly transforming the foundation of modern infrastructure and driving unprecedented advancements across public and private sectors. As SI systems become increasingly integrated into critical operations, they introduce a complex landscape of potential vulnerabilities, both as a target and as a source or facilitator. Unlike traditional deterministic IT systems, SI models are probabilistic, operate at autonomous machine-speed that vastly outpace human reaction times, and rely on complex development and algorithmic process. This new construction model means that SI systems are not only targets for new types of cyber threats (e.g., data poisoning, prompt injection, model inversion) but can also act as a source of harm through adversarial and non-adversarial malfunctions, hallucinations, or malicious misuse. Consequently, traditional incident response life cycles built for conventional software systems may fracture or be incomplete when applied to SI, making the updating of incident response in the presence of SI a priority.

原本 PDF p.9

超知能（SI）1は、現代のインフラの基盤を急速に変えつつあり、官民の各分野で前例のない進歩を推し進めています。SI システムが重要な業務にますます組み込まれるにつれ、SI システムは、標的としても、発生源または促進要因としても、潜在的な脆弱性の複雑な様相をもたらします。従来の決定論的な IT システムとは異なり、SI モデルは確率的であり、人間の反応時間を大きく上回る自律的な機械速度で動作し、複雑な開発およびアルゴリズムのプロセスに依存しています。この新しい構築モデルは、SI システムが新しい種類のサイバー脅威（例：データポイズニング、プロンプトインジェクション、モデル反転）の標的となるだけでなく、敵対的および非敵対的な誤動作、ハルシネーション、または悪意のある悪用を通じて、危害の発生源にもなり得ることを意味します。その結果、従来のソフトウェアシステム向けに作られたインシデント対応ライフサイクルは、SI に適用すると破綻したり不完全になったりするおそれがあり、SI が存在する状況でのインシデント対応の更新が優先課題となっています。

3. 基調講演 — 「AI インシデント管理に関するグローバルな視点：リスク、対応、ギャップ」、Prof. Neil Thompson（MIT FutureTech）

In his keynote address, Dr. Neil Thompson framed AI incident management as a problem of risk prioritization, evidence, organizational preparedness, and responsibility across the AI value chain. His keynote focused on three questions: which AI risks matter most, how companies are responding, and where major management gaps remain. He introduced the MIT AI Risk Initiative, including the AI Risk Repository, AI Risk Index, AI Mitigations Repository, AI Incident Tracking, and AI Governance Mapping. These resources are intended to help organizations identify AI risks, prioritize them, understand available mitigations, track real-world harms, and map relevant governance mechanisms.

原本 PDF p.13

基調講演で Dr. Neil Thompson は、AI インシデント管理を、リスクの優先付け、証拠、組織の備え、そして AI バリューチェーン全体にわたる責任の問題として位置づけました。講演は三つの問いに焦点を当てていました。どの AI リスクが最も重要か、企業はどう対応しているか、そして管理上の大きなギャップはどこに残っているか、です。彼は MIT AI Risk Initiative を紹介し、その中には AI Risk Repository、AI Risk Index、AI Mitigations Repository、AI Incident Tracking、AI Governance Mapping が含まれます。これらのリソースは、組織が AI リスクを特定して優先付けし、利用可能な緩和策を理解し、現実世界の危害を追跡し、関連するガバナンスの仕組みを対応付けるのに役立つことを意図しています。

Dr. Thompson discussed recent research from the project, which convened 272 professional and academic experts from 37 countries in a three-round survey to identify areas of agreement. These experts assessed AI risk by rating their concern, estimating probabilities for different levels of harm, identifying vulnerable actors and sectors, and assigning responsibility for mitigation. The results of the survey suggest that the most severe predicted AI risks over the next five years will be AI used for cyber attacks, weapons development, and mass harm; AI systems with dangerous capabilities; and competitive dynamics that may cause companies to compromise on safety and responsibility.

Even if pragmatic mitigations are adopted, experts still predict that five of the 24 risks are more than 10 % likely to cause catastrophic outcomes. These include dangerous AI capabilities (e.g., systems that can deceive, manipulate, or act autonomously in harmful ways), AI-enabled cyber attacks, environmental harm, inequality, unemployment, and power centralization (i.e., control over advanced AI becomes concentrated among a few firms or governments).

Referencing a 2025 Replit Agent incident, he explained that containment must now rely on “time-to-revoke” primitives and “soft isolation.” Furthermore, recovery is complicated by the fact that surgically removing poisoned data (i.e., machine unlearning) is incredibly difficult, and some AI vulnerabilities simply cannot be patched in the traditional sense, a reality highlighted by the 2018 Amazon Recruiting and 2025 Anthropic GTG-1002 cases.

Finally, Lambros addressed the Post-Incident Analysis phase, which is heavily fractured by what he termed the “reproducibility envelope.” Unlike traditional malware, AI models are non-deterministic; a prompt that triggers a data leak today might yield a completely benign response tomorrow. This unpinned state makes investigating an attack impossible unless specific preparation work is done beforehand. To operationalize these fixes, Lambros left the audience with a strict 90-day action plan: within the first 30 days, organizations must add reasoning-layer logging to every AI deployment runbook; by day 60, they must measure and aggressively cut their time-to-revoke for agent authorizations; and by day 90, they must build comprehensive “soft isolation” playbooks to contain threats without breaking deeply integrated business systems.

A central theme, emphasized especially by Madhu Srikumar, was that AI value chains fragment both visibility and responsibility. A foundation model provider may see model-level behavior; a platform or API provider may see service activity; a framework or harness provider may see execution traces; a deployer may see inputs, outputs, business context, and user impact; and users or affected parties may be the first to observe harm. The panelists also emphasized that AI agents complicate responsibility because the operational risk of an agent is not determined by the foundation model alone. The panel also stressed that mature end-to-end AI incident response capabilities are not yet widely in place. Existing cybersecurity practices provide useful building blocks, but AI-agent incidents may unfold across multiple steps, systems, organizations, and time horizons. 8.1. Track 1: AI Systems as the Target of Cybersecurity Incidents

原本 PDF p.33

8.1. トラック 1: サイバーセキュリティインシデントの標的としての AI システム

The in-person cybersecurity breakout focused on incidents in which the AI system is itself the target or victim of a cybersecurity or information-security event. Participants identified AI systems as new targets and attack surfaces, including compromised or manipulated agents, credential harvesting and privilege escalation in agent environments, AI-assisted phishing, shadow AI deployments, AI-enabled vulnerability discovery and exploitation, and cloud-native ephemeral workloads that may disappear before investigators can understand what happened. Participants emphasized that core security practices (i.e., system hardening, benchmarking, logging, identity and access management) remain essential. However, these practices must be extended to agents, harnesses, models, AI-specific infrastructure, and non-human identities.

原本 PDF p.33

対面のサイバーセキュリティ分科会は、AI システム自体がサイバーセキュリティまたは情報セキュリティ事象の標的または被害者となるインシデントに焦点を当てました。参加者は、AI システムを新たな標的および攻撃対象領域として挙げ、その例として、侵害または操作されたエージェント、エージェント環境における認証情報の収集と権限昇格、AI を利用したフィッシング、シャドー AI の展開、AI を利用した脆弱性の発見と悪用、調査担当者が何が起きたかを理解する前に消失しうるクラウドネイティブな一時的ワークロードを挙げました。参加者は、システムのハードニング、ベンチマーク、ログ記録、アイデンティティ・アクセス管理といった中核的なセキュリティ実務が引き続き不可欠であると強調しました。ただし、これらの実務は、エージェント、ハーネス、モデル、AI 固有のインフラストラクチャ、人間以外のアイデンティティにまで拡張しなければなりません。

The virtual cybersecurity breakouts reinforced similar concerns. Participants highlighted risks associated with shadow MCP servers, “vibe-coded” applications created outside IT oversight, exposed API keys and credentials, data spills, insecure AI-generated code, and uncertain liability hand-offs among model developers, deployers, and users. Across these discussions, visibility emerged as the prerequisite for all later response activity. Organizations cannot apply threat modeling, identify stakeholders, preserve evidence, or respond effectively to systems that security teams do not know exist.

原本 PDF p.33

オンラインのサイバーセキュリティ分科会でも、同様の懸念が強調されました。参加者は、シャドー MCP サーバー、IT の監督外で作成された「バイブコーディング」アプリケーション、公開された API キーや認証情報、データ流出、安全でない AI 生成コード、モデル開発者、展開者、ユーザーの間での責任の引き継ぎの不確かさに伴うリスクを挙げました。これらの議論を通じて、可視性が、その後のあらゆる対応活動の前提条件として浮かび上がりました。組織は、セキュリティチームがその存在を知らないシステムに対して、脅威モデリングを適用することも、関係者を特定することも、証拠を保全することも、効果的に対応することもできません。

Cybersecurity practitioners and AI practitioners often use the same terms differently (e.g., “red teaming” may mean penetration testing to cyber professionals but guardrail bypass testing to AI professionals). Cyber responders may understand traditional artifacts, logs, and malware investigation but lack familiarity with the AI pipeline from data ingestion through model training, deployment, retrieval, inference, and agent tool use. This gap complicates triage, evidence collection, root-cause analysis, and coordination during incidents.

8.2. Track 2: Misuse, Malfunction, and Harm-Based Incidents

原本 PDF p.34

8.2. トラック 2: 悪用、誤動作、危害に基づくインシデント

The in-person harm-focused breakout emphasized that AI incident prioritization depends heavily on context and consequence. Participants discussed categories of harm (e.g., privacy breaches, financial harm, discrimination, client-facing errors, infrastructure damage, property damage, rights-related harms, malicious use, agentic behavior) that may affect safety-critical environments. A recurring point was that a technically secure AI system can still create an AI incident if it produces incorrect outputs, enables unacceptable decisions, or causes harm in a specific operational context.

原本 PDF p.34

対面の危害重視の分科会では、AI インシデントの優先順位付けが文脈と結果に大きく左右されることが強調されました。参加者は、安全性が重要な環境に影響しうる危害のカテゴリ（例: プライバシー侵害、金銭的危害、差別、顧客向けのエラー、インフラへの損害、財産への損害、権利に関連する危害、悪意ある使用、エージェント型の挙動）について議論しました。繰り返し出た論点は、技術的に安全な AI システムであっても、誤った出力を生成したり、受け入れがたい意思決定を可能にしたり、特定の運用上の文脈で危害を引き起こしたりすれば、AI インシデントを生じうるという点でした。

Participants also discussed the difficulty of identifying AI incidents before human users recognize that something is wrong. AI systems can behave probabilistically, may not reproduce the same failure twice, and may be manipulated through rare, context-dependent, or adversarially selected inputs. Participants raised the possibility of AI-assisted or agentic defenses operating at machine speed, particularly in scenarios in which human responders cannot keep pace with automated attacks. At the same time, they recognized that governance, authorization, provenance, and accountability mechanisms are not yet mature enough to support fully automated response without careful safeguards.

原本 PDF p.34

参加者はまた、人間のユーザーが何かがおかしいと気づく前に AI インシデントを特定することの難しさについても議論しました。AI システムは確率的に動作する場合があり、同じ故障を二度再現しないこともあり、まれな、文脈依存の、または敵対的に選ばれた入力によって操作される場合があります。参加者は、特に人間の対応者が自動化された攻撃に追いつけないシナリオにおいて、機械の速度で動作する AI 支援型またはエージェント型の防御の可能性を提起しました。同時に、ガバナンス、認可、来歴、説明責任の仕組みは、慎重な保護措置なしに完全自動化された対応を支えるにはまだ成熟していないことも認識しました。

The harm-focused discussion also underscored that existing taxonomies and frameworks do not always map neatly to AI harms. Participants noted that AI incidents may combine cybersecurity, privacy, safety, consumer protection, civil rights, and sector-specific regulatory concerns. This creates a need for crosswalks among existing frameworks and clearer guidance on how harm-based incidents should be categorized, prioritized, escalated, and remediated.

原本 PDF p.34

危害重視の議論ではまた、既存の分類体系やフレームワークが AI の危害に常にうまく対応づけられるわけではないことも強調されました。参加者は、AI インシデントが、サイバーセキュリティ、プライバシー、安全性、消費者保護、公民権、分野固有の規制上の懸念を併せ持つ場合があると指摘しました。このため、既存のフレームワーク間の対応表と、危害に基づくインシデントをどのように分類し、優先順位付けし、エスカレーションし、是正するかについての、より明確なガイダンスが必要となります。

•Machine-speed response and human governance. Participants repeatedly raised the concern that AI-enabled attacks or agentic failures may unfold faster than traditional human-centered response processes.
